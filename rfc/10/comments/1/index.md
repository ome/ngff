## Summary

This comment asks the RFC to name the initial members of the Editorial Board and explain how they have been selected, and endorses Josh Moore and Virginie Uhlmann as members of the Editorial Board.

## Significant comments and questions

### Selection of the initial Editorial Board members

RFC-10 describes in detail how the Editorial Board manages its membership going forward, with ratification by the OMG.
It does not, however, specify who the initial members of the Editorial Board are, presumably the RFC authors, and the reference to the Editorial Board Roster is still TBD.
Since the RFC establishes the Editorial Board, I would ask the authors to explicitly list the initial members, including the initial Managing Editor, in the RFC itself.
Additionally, since the initial composition sets the tone for the Board and, through its self-management, also shapes its future composition, I would ask the authors to make the selection process and the criteria that are meaningful for Board membership more explicit.
The RFC currently only refers to "sustained and demonstrable operational involvement", which leaves a lot of room for interpretation.
It is understandable that the selection of the initial members differs from the regular process, since there is no Board yet to propose and approve members.
Nevertheless, the initial selection process should also be explained.

### Endorsement of Josh Moore

I would like to endorse Josh as a member of the Editorial Board.

Josh is an obvious choice: he has served as the sole Editor under RFC-1 and has been driving a lot of the progress in OME-Zarr.
Continuity in this role is valuable.

### Endorsement of Virginie Uhlmann

I would like to endorse Virginie as a member of the Editorial Board.

Virginie is leading many of the community-building efforts around OME-Zarr, including symposia, hackathons, and community meetings.
This work is just as important as the technical work.
Creating a technical format and writing a specification is achievable for many, even more so now with AI agents.
The main value of OME-Zarr, however, is that it is a format agreed upon by large parts of the community.
Achieving and maintaining that agreement requires a great deal of trust within the community, and Virginie's work directly builds that trust.

Additionally, Virginie brings experience with editorial tasks as well as strong scientific credentials, both of which are well suited for the responsibilities of the Editorial Board.

I would suggest that the RFC explicitly recognize community building as a valid form of sustained engagement that qualifies for Editorial Board membership.
